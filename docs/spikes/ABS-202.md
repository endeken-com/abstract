# ABS-202: ABEL, a voice assistant that hears and speaks on the Mac

**Go.** ABEL ships as a helper app inside Abstract, `Abel.app`. Push-to-talk comes first, and
the wake phrase is there too, off by default. ABEL needs macOS 26 or later; Abstract itself
stays on macOS 15. Nothing ABEL hears or says leaves the Mac, and Abstract still calls no
model.

**Method.** Probed 2026-11-17 on an M1 Pro (10 cores, 16 GB) running macOS 27.2 with Xcode
26.5, plugged in. The test audio was made with `say`. For English: Samantha, Eddy, Flo,
Shelley, Sandy, Kathy, Fred, Albert and Daniel (en-GB). For Portuguese: Luciana and the
Brazilian Flo, Eddy, Shelley and Sandy. It was run through Apple's on-device recognizers
from files, with no microphone. Small throwaway Swift programs did the probing: a harness
for the recognizers, a stream that fed audio at real speed, a speech-synthesis probe, and a
two-app bundle for the panel, the hotkey and the login item. None of that code is kept.
Synthetic voices are harder to recognise than people (Fred and Albert are novelty voices),
so treat every accuracy figure below as a floor. The figures for real voices are among the
manual checks at the end.

## 1. Waking it

| Option | Result here |
|---|---|
| `SpeechTranscriber` (macOS 26), streaming | **Chosen.** Runs entirely on the Mac. Needs **no Speech Recognition permission**: it transcribed files while that permission was still unasked. The microphone permission is still needed for live audio. |
| `SFSpeechRecognizer`, on-device | Needs the Speech Recognition permission (it would prompt, so it wasn't run). It reports `supportsOnDeviceRecognition == false` for pt-BR on this Mac, which would send Portuguese audio to Apple. **Ruled out**, and with it ABEL on macOS 15. |
| Bundled keyword-spotting model | Not tried. It would need a trained model for "Hey Abel" and "Ei Abel", its licence, and a runtime to load it. Worth revisiting only if real-world false wakes are too frequent. |
| Push-to-talk, ⌥Space | `RegisterEventHotKey` (Carbon) registered ⌥Space with no permission at all, and reports both press and release. **Ships first.** |
| Push-to-talk, holding Fn | Needs Input Monitoring. Not offered. |

**The wake rule.** Wake when a sentence starts with up to three greetings ("hey", "okay",
"ei", "oi", "a"…) followed by the word "Abel". After a greeting, "able" also counts, but only
when nothing follows it or a command word does. In Portuguese a bare "Abel" at the start of
a sentence counts too, because the recognizer often drops "Ei". One rule covers both
languages; the recognizer runs in whichever language you speak (English, Portuguese, or
both).

| Measured | English | Portuguese |
|---|---|---|
| Wake phrases detected | 8 of 10 (the two misses were the novelty voices Fred and Albert) | 4 of 6 |
| Near misses ignored ("a bell", "able to", "label", "Hey Abby", "Cain and Abel", "abelha", "hábil", "O Abel ligou", "Ei, Abílio"…) | 10 of 10 | 5 of 5 |
| False wakes in continuous speech packed with traps | 1 in 17 min ("Okay, able, that's enough for today", heard as "Okay, Abel") | 0 in 7 min |
| Time to wake, streamed at real speed | before the speaker finished, to 0.1 s after | as English |

Recognition took 13 s of wall time for 1,008 s of continuous English audio, or 77 times
faster than real time. Listening live therefore keeps the recognizer busy about 1.3% of the
time. ABEL's own process used 0.2% to 0.7% of a core per second of audio, and the rest runs
in Apple's speech service. Battery can't be measured on mains power without root
`powermetrics`; with a busy share that low, it is a manual check. The Portuguese model was
downloaded once through `AssetInventory` (10.8 s). That fetches Apple's model; no audio
goes up.

## 2. Hearing

The test set was 50 developer phrases: 45 in English, 5 in Portuguese, read by 4 voices.
They cover commands, chat and project names, "PR 43", branch and file names, and agent
names.

| Engine | Word error | Exact | Median latency after speech | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SpeechTranscriber` | 30.7% | 23/50 | 0.17 s | 0.26 s |
| `DictationTranscriber` with contextual strings | 30.3% | 25/50 | 0.55 s | 1.21 s |

* Command words come through ("what needs me", "approve", "stop", "read the last reply"),
  and so do plain names ("Gemini transport", "nightly catalog"). Code-shaped words don't:
  "xcodebuild" was heard as "the scattermill", "Muse" as "military", "StoreMigration" as
  "store migration".
* **Biasing.** Contextual strings (`AnalysisContext`) changed nothing for
  `SpeechTranscriber`: all 31 wake and near-miss files came back identical. They helped
  `DictationTranscriber` with names ("palette", "plugin", "Worktree naming"), but that engine
  dropped whole clauses, especially in Portuguese, and was three times slower. **Decision:**
  `SpeechTranscriber` for everything. The bias toward this Mac's names happens *after*
  recognition, in the matcher (section 4), which compares what was heard against this Mac's
  project, chat and agent names. ABEL still passes those names as contextual strings, which
  costs nothing and helps if a later macOS honours them.

## 3. Speaking

* `AVSpeechSynthesizer` writing into a buffer. A warm start took 193 ms to the first audio,
  for 5.8 s of speech; the first call after launch took 1.66 s, so ABEL warms it up at launch.
  This Mac has only the compact voices for en-US (Samantha) and pt-BR (Luciana). Settings
  lists whichever voices are installed. A Personal Voice needs its own permission, which
  ABEL asks for only when you pick one.
* **What's read.** The reply's first paragraph. Code blocks, diffs and tables are replaced by
  one sentence ("…and 40 lines of code"). Long paths are cut to their file name, links to
  their text, and Markdown is stripped. "Continue" reads the next paragraph.
* **Interrupting.** Pressing push-to-talk, or saying the wake phrase, stops speech at once.
  Live barge-in (talking over it) uses the input node's voice processing, which cancels
  ABEL's own echo so it doesn't hear itself.

## 4. Understanding, within the rules

**(a) Instant commands, on the Mac.** These are resolved by a fixed grammar: what needs me,
next one, open ‹chat›, read the last reply, continue, approve, deny, yes, no, stop, pause
everything, status of ‹chat›, start a chat in ‹project› to ‹prompt›, tell ‹chat› ‹text›, send
it, plus their Portuguese forms. Command words match fuzzily (similarity of 0.8 or more,
so "cause everything" still means pause everything). Names match against this Mac's chats,
projects and agents.

The prototype resolved **28 of 31** command transcripts. All three misses had filler words
before the name: "open the chat *about the* plugin catalog", "stop *the agent and* work
rename it". So the matcher drops fillers and looks for the name *inside* the rest, not only
at its start. Anything that matches no command goes to (b).

**(b) ABEL's own chat.** This is an ordinary chat named **ABEL**, pinned, and run by the
agent and profile you pick in Settings › ABEL. Claude, Codex and OpenCode are all
equivalent here. Its tools come from a new server, `abstract mcp --chat <id> --abel`, built
like the plugin-tools server (ABS-97). That server passes each call to Abel.app over a local
socket:

* `list_chats`, `chat_status`, `read_reply`;
* `start_chat`, `send_to_chat`, `stop_chat`, `answer_permission`.

Abel.app reads each acting call back and waits for "yes", then carries it out through
Abstract's local channel. The read-only tools need no "yes". Answers stay in the chat, so
there's history, and ABEL speaks them.

**Foundation Models (for the owner to decide; not built).**
`SystemLanguageModel.default.availability` is `.available` on this Mac. It needs no key, no
account and no network, so it isn't a "model API" in the sense of the rule's wording. It is,
however, a model that Abstract itself would be calling, which goes against the rule's spirit
("agents do the thinking"). What it would add: free phrasings for the instant layer ("how's
the sidebar thing going?" becomes status of Sidebar fix) in well under a second and without
an agent turn, plus one-sentence spoken summaries of long replies. **Recommendation:** allow
it only as an optional switch, off by default, that turns a phrase into one of the grammar's
own commands and never acts on its own. Until the owner says yes, (a) and (b) cover
everything.

## 5. Safety

* An approval is read back ("Allow `rm -rf build` in Sidebar fix? Say yes to confirm") and
  waits for "yes" or "sim". Anything else, or 10 s of silence, cancels it. Every Ask-first
  action works the same way.
* **Never by voice:** merge, push, Full autonomy, deleting a chat or project. ABEL opens the
  screen where you do it instead.
* Microphone and actions stop while the screen is locked. ABEL watches the
  `com.apple.screenIsLocked` notifications and checks `CGSSessionScreenIsLocked`, a key that
  is missing while unlocked, as here.
* Wake and actions also stop while the screen is shared. There's no public API for this:
  ABEL detects macOS Screen Sharing and Remote Management (`screensharingd` running) and
  mirrored displays. It can't see a video call sharing your screen; for that there's
  **Pause ABEL** in its menu.
* ABEL never types into secure fields (`AXSecureTextField`).
* The microphone indicator shows whenever ABEL listens. No audio is written to disk.
* Every action is logged with who asked: "by ABEL" in the target chat, and ABEL's own
  history with the app, the action and the transcript.

## 6. Packaging and install

* **Inside Abstract**, at `Abstract.app/Contents/Library/LoginItems/Abel.app`, rather than
  downloaded from the release. Tested: an ad hoc-signed host app with a helper there
  registered it with `SMAppService.loginItem(identifier:)`, going from `notFound` to
  `enabled`, and unregistered it back to `notRegistered`. No prompt appeared. That means no
  download or separate verification step, the version always matches, and notarisation
  comes with Abstract's. The cost is a few MB in Abstract.app.
* **Install ABEL** in Settings › ABEL registers the login item and launches it. **Remove
  ABEL** unregisters it and quits it; its files stay inside Abstract's signed bundle. On
  macOS 15 the page says ABEL needs macOS 26.
* **Its own bundle id**, `sh.abstract.abel`, with `LSUIElement` and its own usage strings.
  The microphone, Accessibility and Automation permissions are therefore Abel's;
  Abstract never asks. The Speech Recognition permission isn't needed (section 1).
* **Release signing.** `scripts/sign-app.sh` signs Abel.app before Abstract, with the
  hardened runtime and the entitlements `com.apple.security.device.audio-input` and
  `com.apple.security.automation.apple-events`.
* **Gotcha.** Without `-target …-macos26.0`, the swiftc here builds for macOS 28 and
  LaunchServices refuses the app (-10825). Abel's target sets its deployment version
  explicitly.
* **Talking to Abstract.** ABEL uses the `AppLink` socket, with a new `abel` request next to
  `rename`. It's per data folder, so a demo never reaches the real app. If Abstract isn't
  open, ABEL says so and offers to open it.

## 7. The floating panel

A borderless `NSPanel` with `.nonactivatingPanel`, at `.floating` level, with
`canJoinAllSpaces`, `fullScreenAuxiliary` and `stationary` set, `hidesOnDeactivate` off,
and a frame autosave name. Measured: the frontmost app stayed Abstract before and after the
panel appeared. ABEL never became active, and the panel never became key. Drawing it into
an image (`cacheDisplay`) needs no Screen Recording permission, so PRs can carry
screenshots. It shows an orb while idle, and expands while listening ("Listening…" and the
live words) and answering. It can be dragged anywhere. Its menu bar item can show it, hide
it, or pause ABEL.

## 8. Controlling other apps

| Way | Permission | Power | By voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open apps, files and URLs (`NSWorkspace`) | none | low | Allowed |
| The user's Shortcuts (`shortcuts list` / `run`) | none for ABEL; listing worked here | whatever the shortcut does | Ask first by default |
| AppleScript / JXA (Apple Events) | Automation, per app, asked by macOS on first use | high | Ask first; the script is shown and read back |
| Accessibility (`AXUIElement`) | Accessibility (not granted here: `AXIsProcessTrusted() == false`) | highest: read, press, type | Ask first; never into secure fields |

Each app gets **Allowed / Ask first / Off**, Ask first by default, and each kind of control
is off until you turn it on. Anything that sends, buys, deletes, posts or submits needs
"yes", even in an Allowed app. ABEL checks the script or the button's title for those
words. ABEL's chat gets these as tools on the same `--abel` server: `open_app`, `open_url`,
`open_file`, `run_shortcut`, `run_applescript`, `ui_read`, `ui_press` and `ui_type`.

## 9. Where the conversation lives, and the prototype

* While you talk, it lives in the floating panel. The history lives in ABEL's chat in
  Abstract, and Abel's menu bar item has the controls. Push-to-talk on the phone is a later
  ticket.
* **The prototype on this Mac** covered all of it:
  * push-to-talk registered;
  * the grammar over real transcripts, including "what needs me", "open ‹chat›", "approve"
    and "read the last reply";
  * speech synthesised into a buffer;
  * the panel drawn;
  * the login item registered and removed.

  What it couldn't cover: live microphone input, the Speech, Accessibility and Automation
  permissions, and real voices. The ABEL PRs list those as manual checks for the morning.
